Constraints and dimensions

sk.Constrain(a, b, kind, value, id=...) adds a constraint between one or two references, read from a to b: Constrain("line", "circle", px.Tangent) makes the line tangent to the circle. A reference can be

  • an element by its id, such as "bottom";

  • a point of an element, such as "bottom.start", "bottom.end", "bottom.mid" or "fillet.center";

  • a point made with Vertex;

  • the sketch’s axes px.H and px.V, or its origin sk.origin.

The geometric kinds also work as flags of the drawing calls (see Drawing with constraints). The dimensions take a value and usually an id, so that they can be changed later.

Kind

Between

Meaning

Coincident

A point and a point, line, circle or spline

The point lies on the other reference

Horizontal, Vertical

A line, or two points

Along px.H or px.V

Parallel, Perpendicular

Two lines

Parallel, or at a right angle

Tangent

Two of line, arc, circle and spline

Touching, on the side as drawn

Equal

Two lines, or two circles and arcs

Equal lengths or radii

Symmetric

Two points and a line

Mirror images of each other

SymmetricCarriers

Two elements and a line

The mirror image of one element’s line or circle is the other’s

Concentric

Two circles or arcs

One common center

Fix

A point or an element

Stays where it is

Distance

A line’s length, or points and lines

A dimension

Radius, Diameter

A circle or arc

A dimension

Angle

Two lines, or a line and an axis

A dimension in degrees

RigidGroup

Elements

Keep their shape and move as one

 
[1]:

import planex as px

A plate with a hole, all constraints shown

The example is the plate from the section overview, with a hole of diameter 10, 15 from the bottom and 20 from the left side. show_constraints places a sign next to every constraint: a circle at a coincidence, a square in a perpendicular corner, H and V, two short strokes along a tangent, an anchor for a fixed point, and the value of each dimension with its id (Figure 1a). show_ends marks the start of every line and arc in blue and its end in orange, which is what .start and .end refer to, and show_direction adds arrows (Figure 1b). sk.Plot(show_constraints=True) draws the same with matplotlib. With backend="plotly", the picture is interactive, and every sign is explained on hover.

[3]:

sk = px.Sketch(id="plate")
sk.Move(sk.origin, px.Coincident)
sk.Line(37, 2.5, px.Horizontal, id="bottom")
sk.Arc(9, 95, px.Tangent, id="fillet")
sk.Line(-1, 17, px.Vertical, px.Tangent, id="right")
sk.Line(-46, 3, px.Perpendicular, id="top")
sk.Close(px.Perpendicular, id="left")
sk.Constrain("fillet", px.Radius, 10, id="r")
sk.Constrain("bottom.start", "right", px.Distance, 50, id="width")
sk.Constrain("bottom", "top", px.Distance, 30, id="height")

sk.Circle(20, 15, 5, id="hole")
sk.Constrain("hole", px.Diameter, 10, id="d")
sk.Constrain("hole.center", "bottom", px.Distance, 15, id="y_hole")
sk.Constrain("hole.center", "left", px.Distance, 20, id="x_hole")
print(sk.status, sk.dof, sorted(sk.dimensions))

full 0 ['d', 'height', 'r', 'width', 'x_hole', 'y_hole']

Reference dimensions

A dimension with reference=True and no value drives nothing; it only measures. sk["diagonal"] is the distance from the plate’s origin to the hole’s center in the current solution, and it follows every change.

[5]:

sk.Constrain(sk.origin, "hole.center", px.Distance, id="diagonal", reference=True)
print("diagonal", sk["diagonal"], "exact", (20**2 + 15**2) ** 0.5)
sk.Update({"x_hole": 30, "y_hole": 10})
print("diagonal", sk["diagonal"], "exact", (30**2 + 10**2) ** 0.5, sk.status)

diagonal 25.0 exact 25.0
diagonal 31.622776601683793 exact 31.622776601683793 full

Removing a constraint

Remove(id) removes a constraint, and the element it held can move again. Without the hole’s diameter, the sketch has one degree of freedom left.

[6]:

sk.Remove("d")
print("without d:", sk.status, sk.dof, "degree of freedom")
sk.Constrain("hole", px.Radius, 5, id="d")
print("with d again:", sk.status, sk.dof, "degrees of freedom")

without d: under 1 degree of freedom
with d again: full 0 degrees of freedom
