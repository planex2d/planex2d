Fields and cuts

A simulation result means little without its geometry. It matters where the hot spot is, which wall carries the flux, and what the field looks like in a plane through the bore. px.PlotField(field, mesh, obj) draws a field on the planex2d geometry it was computed on, in 2D, with matplotlib or plotly, and px.PlotMesh(mesh, obj) draws the mesh. The field can come from NGSolve, as a GridFunction or any CoefficientFunction, or from FEniCSx, as a dolfinx Function. The examples on this page use NGSolve.

Call or keyword

What it draws

px.PlotField(field, obj)

The field as colors over the faces (its magnitude for vectors)

px.PlotField(field, mesh, obj)

The same for a CoefficientFunction, evaluated on mesh

px.PlotMesh(mesh, obj)

The element edges, curved where the mesh is curved

vectors=True

Arrows of a vector field

field_lines=True or a density

The field lines of a vector field

contours=n or a list

Contour lines at n levels or at the given values

min=, max=

The color range, as in NGSolve’s Draw

colormap=

"default" (the planex2d map, from low in red to high in blue), "default_r", any matplotlib name, or a list of colors

A 3D result can be drawn in any plane. Either cut the solid with px.Intersect3d and plot the field on the cut, or pass the cutting plane to px.PlotField as obj.

Every value shown is the field at that pixel’s point, with no interpolation between mesh nodes, and only where both the geometry and the mesh are present, so a pixel that the mesh does not cover stays empty. Every section ends with a check against a known number.

[1]:

import math
import time

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import netgen.occ as occ
import ngsolve as ngs
import numpy as np
import planex as px
import planex.measurements as pm

mm = px.mm


def solve(fes, a, f=None, boundary=None):
    gf = ngs.GridFunction(fes)
    if boundary is not None:
        gf.Set(boundary, ngs.BND)
    a.Assemble()
    r = -a.mat * gf.vec
    if f is not None:
        f.Assemble()
        r += f.vec
    gf.vec.data += a.mat.Inverse(fes.FreeDofs()) * r
    return gf

The helper solve has NGSolve solve a(u, v) = f(v) with the Dirichlet values boundary, a CoefficientFunction on the boundary.

A temperature field on a part

The example is an aluminum cooling plate, 120 × 60 mm, with k = 200 W/(m K). A 30 mm heater pad on its bottom edge puts in 400 W per meter of depth, two coolant channels at 20 °C take it out, and all other edges are insulated. The edges carry their names from the sketch, so the boundary conditions refer to "heater" and "coolant", not to edge numbers.

[3]:

k = 200.0
P = 400.0

s = px.QuickSketch().Line(45 * mm, name="air").Line(30 * mm, name="heater").Line(45 * mm, name="air")
s.Line(0, 60 * mm, name="air").Line(-120 * mm, 0, name="air").Close(name="air")
s.Circle(30 * mm, 40 * mm, 6 * mm, name="coolant").Circle(90 * mm, 40 * mm, 6 * mm, name="coolant")
plate = s.Face()

mesh = ngs.Mesh(px.OCCGeometry(plate).GenerateMesh(maxh=4 * mm))
mesh.Curve(3)
fes = ngs.H1(mesh, order=3, dirichlet="coolant")
T, v = fes.TnT()
a = ngs.BilinearForm(k * ngs.grad(T) * ngs.grad(v) * ngs.dx)
f = ngs.LinearForm(P / (30 * mm) * v * ngs.ds("heater"))
gfT = solve(fes, a, f, mesh.BoundaryCF({"coolant": 20.0}, default=0))
print(mesh.ne, "elements, boundaries", sorted(set(mesh.GetBoundaries())))

999 elements, boundaries ['air', 'coolant', 'heater']

The temperature is shown with 12 contour lines, next to the mesh it was computed on. The planex2d color map runs from red (low) to blue (high), the convention of these docs, where low values are critical, as for a thin wall. For a temperature, hot should be red, so these plots use colormap="default_r". px.PlotMesh draws the element edges, curved like the mesh.

[4]:

fig, (left, right) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 3.6))
px.PlotField(gfT, plate, ax=left, colormap="default_r", contours=12)
left.set_title("Temperature (°C), 12 contours")
px.PlotMesh(mesh, plate, ax=right, legend=False)
right.set_title("The mesh, curved to order 3")
plt.show()

The heat flux -k grad T is a vector field, and the color shows its magnitude. vectors=True adds arrows, and field_lines=True adds the lines the heat follows from the pad into the channels. A CoefficientFunction like this one has no mesh of its own, so the mesh is the second argument of px.PlotField.

Check: what the pad puts in must leave through the channels. On a boundary, NGSolve’s grad is the gradient along the boundary, so the normal flux needs BoundaryFromVolumeCF.

[5]:

flux = -k * ngs.grad(gfT)
shown = {"colormap": "default_r"}
fig, (left, right) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 3.6))
px.PlotField(flux, mesh, plate, ax=left, vectors=True, **shown)
left.set_title("Heat flux (W/m²) with arrows")
px.PlotField(flux, mesh, plate, ax=right, field_lines=True, **shown)
right.set_title("Heat flux with field lines")
plt.show()

n = ngs.specialcf.normal(2)
normal_flux = ngs.InnerProduct(ngs.BoundaryFromVolumeCF(flux), n)
out = ngs.Integrate(normal_flux, mesh, definedon=mesh.Boundaries("coolant"))
print("in through the heater", P, "W/m, out through the channels", round(out, 2), "W/m")
print("difference", abs(out - P) / P, "of the power")

An exact check: a pipe wall

A pipe wall between the radii 10 and 25 mm, at 90 °C inside and 20 °C outside, has the exact temperature T(r) = T_i + (T_o − T_i) ln(r / r_i) / ln(r_o / r_i). The plot shows the error of the finite element solution, gf - exact, a CoefficientFunction like any other. With a symmetric min and max the default map works as a diverging one, where the cream middle means no error, red too cold and blue too warm.

The walls are circles, so it matters how well the mesh follows them. A straight mesh (Curve(1)) replaces each circle by a polygon. Between the polygon and the true circle, there is geometry but no mesh, and those pixels stay empty instead of showing an extrapolated value. A curved mesh (Curve(3)) fills the circle.

[6]:

ri, ro = 10 * mm, 25 * mm
Ti, To = 90.0, 20.0
wall = px.QuickSketch().Circle(0, 0, ro, name="outside").Circle(0, 0, ri, name="inside").Face()
r = ngs.sqrt(ngs.x ** 2 + ngs.y ** 2)
exact = Ti + (To - Ti) * ngs.log(r / ri) / math.log(ro / ri)


def pipe_solution(curve):
    m = ngs.Mesh(px.OCCGeometry(wall).GenerateMesh(maxh=3 * mm))
    m.Curve(curve)
    space = ngs.H1(m, order=3, dirichlet="inside|outside")
    u, w = space.TnT()
    a = ngs.BilinearForm(ngs.grad(u) * ngs.grad(w) * ngs.dx)
    held = m.BoundaryCF({"inside": Ti, "outside": To})
    return m, solve(space, a, boundary=held)


def empty_pixels(ax, shape):
    image = ax.get_images()[0]
    z = np.ma.filled(image.get_array().astype(float), np.nan)
    x0, x1, y0, y1 = image.get_extent()
    xs = x0 + (np.arange(z.shape[1]) + 0.5) * (x1 - x0) / z.shape[1]
    ys = y0 + (np.arange(z.shape[0]) + 0.5) * (y1 - y0) / z.shape[0]
    gx, gy = np.meshgrid(xs, ys)
    points = np.c_[gx.ravel(), gy.ravel()]
    inside = shape.Contains(points, boundary=False).reshape(z.shape)
    return int((inside & np.isnan(z)).sum()), int(inside.sum())


fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 5))
for ax, curve, limit in zip(axes, (1, 3), (0.5, 0.005)):
    m, gf = pipe_solution(curve)
    rms = math.sqrt(ngs.Integrate((gf - exact) ** 2, m) / ngs.Integrate(1, m))
    px.PlotField(gf - exact, m, wall, ax=ax, min=-limit, max=limit)
    empty, total = empty_pixels(ax, wall)
    ax.set_title(f"Curve({curve}): error in K, range ±{limit}, "
                 f"rms {rms:.1e} K\n"
                 f"{empty} of {total} pixels in the wall empty")
plt.show()

empty_pixels counts the pixels whose center lies in the wall but that show nothing. Zoomed in on the inner wall of the straight mesh, the empty crescents are where the polygon cuts the circle, and no value is invented there. px.PlotMesh on the same axes draws the mesh on top.

Check: the heat flows radially, so every field line must point at the center. The lines are the ones px.PlotField drew, which matplotlib keeps in the axes; the angle between each piece of a line and the radius is measured.

[8]:

m3, gf3 = pipe_solution(3)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(6, 5))
px.PlotField(-ngs.grad(gf3), m3, wall, ax=ax, colormap="default_r", field_lines=1.5)
ax.set_title("The temperature gradient: field lines")
plt.show()

lines = [c for c in ax.collections if c.get_gid() == "field lines"][0]
worst, steps = 0.0, 0
for line in lines.get_segments():
    for p, q in zip(line[:-1], line[1:]):
        d, mid = q - p, (p + q) / 2
        if np.hypot(*d) > 1e-9:
            steps += 1
            cross = d[0] * mid[1] - d[1] * mid[0]
            sine = cross / np.hypot(*d) / np.hypot(*mid)
            worst = max(worst, math.degrees(abs(math.asin(sine))))
print(steps, "steps along the field lines, largest angle to the radius", round(worst, 3), "degrees")

min and max at a re-entrant corner

An L-shaped plate is held at 0 on its long bottom edge and at 1 on the top of the short leg. At the inner corner, the gradient is singular: it grows like r^(−1/3) towards the corner, without bound as the mesh is refined there. plate_L.vertices(near=corner).maxh gives the corner vertex a small mesh size, which goes onto the OpenCASCADE vertex, and netgen grades the mesh towards it. With the color range taken from the values, the corner is all that can be seen. max= fixes the range, so the rest of the plate gets the colors, and the contours follow the same range.

[9]:

s = px.QuickSketch().Line(40 * mm, 0, name="cold").Line(0, 20 * mm).Line(-20 * mm, 0)
plate_L = s.Line(0, 20 * mm).Line(-20 * mm, 0, name="hot").Close().Face()
plate_L.vertices(near=(20 * mm, 20 * mm)).maxh = 0.05 * mm
mesh_L = ngs.Mesh(px.OCCGeometry(plate_L).GenerateMesh(maxh=2 * mm))
space_L = ngs.H1(mesh_L, order=3, dirichlet="cold|hot")
u, w = space_L.TnT()
a = ngs.BilinearForm(ngs.grad(u) * ngs.grad(w) * ngs.dx)
u_L = solve(space_L, a, boundary=mesh_L.BoundaryCF({"hot": 1.0}, default=0))
gradient = ngs.Norm(ngs.grad(u_L))

shown = {"colormap": "default_r", "contours": 8}
fig, (left, right) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 5))
px.PlotField(gradient, mesh_L, plate_L, ax=left, **shown)
left.set_title("|grad u|, the color range from the values")
px.PlotField(gradient, mesh_L, plate_L, ax=right, max=40, **shown)

plt.show()

values = np.ma.filled(left.get_images()[0].get_array().astype(float), np.nan)

      round(np.nanpercentile(values, 99)))

Cutting a 3D part

The example is an aluminum block, 80 × 40 × 30 mm, with a bore of 6 mm radius held at 80 °C. Its right face sits on a heat sink at 20 °C, and the other faces lose heat to the air at 15 W/(m² K). The result lives on a tetrahedral mesh. To look inside, the solid is cut with px.Intersect3d. The cut is a planex2d shape on the cutting plane, and px.PlotField evaluates the 3D field at each pixel’s 3D point. px.PlotMesh with the 3D mesh draws where the plane cuts the elements.

[10]:

block = occ.Box(occ.Pnt(0, 0, 0), occ.Pnt(80 * mm, 40 * mm, 30 * mm))
bore = occ.Cylinder(occ.Pnt(20 * mm, 20 * mm, 0), occ.Z, r=6 * mm, h=30 * mm)
part = block - bore
part.faces.name = "air"
part.faces.Nearest(occ.Pnt(26 * mm, 20 * mm, 15 * mm)).name = "bore"
part.faces.Max(occ.X).name = "sink"

mesh3 = ngs.Mesh(occ.OCCGeometry(part).GenerateMesh(maxh=4 * mm))
mesh3.Curve(2)
space3 = ngs.H1(mesh3, order=2, dirichlet="bore|sink")
u, w = space3.TnT()
a = ngs.BilinearForm(200 * ngs.grad(u) * ngs.grad(w) * ngs.dx + 15 * u * w * ngs.ds("air"))
held = mesh3.BoundaryCF({"bore": 80.0, "sink": 20.0}, default=0)
T3 = solve(space3, a, boundary=held)
print(mesh3.ne, "tetrahedra")

6794 tetrahedra

The cutting plane is a px.Plane through the middle of the block, tilted about the x axis. Every plane argument in planex2d also takes netgen’s occ.Axes, so netgen code can pass its axes unchanged.

[11]:

oblique = px.Plane(origin=(40 * mm, 20 * mm, 15 * mm), normal=(0, 0.6, 1), axis=(1, 0, 0))

Contours on a measurement

contours=, min and max work on every color plot, including the planex2d measurements. The example shows the minimum thickness of an L bracket, the diameter of the largest disc that fits at each point of the boundary, with the color range fixed to the 8 mm wall and the 14 mm corner. A sharp convex corner is infinitely thin by this definition, so ignore_corners=91 leaves out the bracket’s 90° corners.

s = px.QuickSketch().Line(60 * mm, 0).Line(0, 8 * mm).Line(-48 * mm, 0).Line(0, 40 * mm)
bracket = s.Line(-12 * mm, 0).Close().Face()
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(6, 5))
thickness = pm.MinThickness(ignore_corners=91)
px.PlotField(thickness, bracket, ax=ax, contours=5, min=8 * mm, max=14 * mm)
plt.show()

Interactive, and how long it takes

backend="plotly", or a plotly figure as ax, draws the same with zoom and the value under the mouse. As for the cost, every plot evaluates the field once per pixel, with 400 pixels along the longer side by default, and samples= changes that.

for label, draw in plots.items():
    t = time.perf_counter()
    draw()
    plt.close("all")
    print(label, round(time.perf_counter() - t, 2), "s")
